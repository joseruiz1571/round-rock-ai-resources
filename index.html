Key ideas from the talk

Three words

  • Delegated: the tool does it, and you take a quick look.
  • Calibrated: the tool drafts, and you check every line.
  • Retained: you do it yourself, and the tool stays out.

These labels are not permanent. They can change as the tools change, and as you change.

What never goes in the box

Use only public or made-up material when you experiment. Never paste in names, contact information, or other private details about people. If something has private details in it, remove them yourself before it goes anywhere near a tool. The text box will not stop you.

The readiness test

Before you share any draft with a patron, coworker, volunteer, or partner, ask:

  1. Reading level. Would the least confident person follow it?
  2. Assumed knowledge. Is it true here?
  3. The missing step. Where does a first-timer get stuck?
  4. The local fact. What is wrong about our building or rules?
  5. Tone. Does it talk down to people, or past them?
  6. The one thing. Is the most important idea the one that stands out?

Your ten hours

A rule of thumb from researcher Ethan Mollick: give these tools about ten hours of real use before deciding what they can and cannot do for you. To make those hours count:

  • Pick three real tasks.
  • Use public or made-up material only.
  • Try more than one tool on the same task.
  • Check what comes back, and write down where it surprised you.
  • Find a coworker to compare notes with. In three weeks, ask each other: What rough edge did you discover?

The research behind the "jagged frontier"

  • Dell'Acqua, McFowland, Mollick et al., Harvard Business School working paper, 2023
    A study of 758 consultants. With AI, work was rated over 40% higher in quality on tasks inside the tool's range, but people were 19 percentage points more likely to be wrong on a task just outside it.
